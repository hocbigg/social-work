## Introduction

Social work is defined by its dual commitment to individual well-being and structural reform. Unlike disciplines that focus strictly on internal psychological states or broad sociological trends, social work operates at the intersection of both through the **person-in-environment (PIE)** framework. The field approaches human distress not merely as individual pathology, but as the outcome of complex interactions among biological, psychological, familial, community, and political systems. Whether working one-on-one with a person in crisis or organizing for legislative reform, social work practice is explicitly guided by core professional values: human dignity, service, social justice, integrity, competence, and the central importance of human relationships.

This curriculum provides a rigorous, self-directed foundation equivalent to the core academic curriculum of an undergraduate degree in social work (Bachelor of Social Work). It is designed for:

- Independent learners seeking a structured, holistic understanding of human services and social justice.
- Community organizers, mutual aid coordinators, and nonprofit staff looking to ground their grassroots work in established assessment and intervention models.

No prior background in psychology, sociology, or law is assumed. However, self-directed study requires a willingness to engage in candid self-reflection, examine personal biases, and critically analyze systemic inequalities such as racism, poverty, and ableism. 

*Note on professional licensure:* While this curriculum covers the essential theoretical and practical knowledge taught in accredited social work programs, self-study does not confer formal academic credit, legal licensure (such as LSW or LCSW credentials), or the supervised agency field practicum hours legally required to practice licensed clinical social work.

### How the Curriculum Is Structured

The eleven subjects below form a coherent generalist learning sequence. Rather than treating subjects as isolated topics, move through them in four progressive stages:

- **Ethical and Theoretical Foundations:** Begin with *Introduction to Social Work and Social Welfare*, *Social Work Values and Ethics*, and *Diversity, Social Justice, and Human Rights*. These subjects establish the profession's history, professional boundaries, and anti-oppressive frameworks. They are mandatory prerequisites for understanding everything that follows.
- **Human Development and Relational Skills:** Next, work through *Human Behavior in the Social Environment* and *Interpersonal Communication and Interviewing Skills*. These teach you to assess human functioning across the lifespan within ecological systems and master the micro-level communication skills (active listening, rapport building, intake interviewing) required for direct client engagement.
- **The Generalist Intervention Model (Micro, Mezzo, Macro):** Study the three practice tiers: *Generalist Social Work Practice: Individuals and Families*, *Generalist Social Work Practice: Groups*, and *Generalist Practice: Organizations and Communities*. These subjects apply a unified problem-solving process—engagement, assessment, planning, intervention, evaluation, and termination—across individual cases, group dynamics, and neighborhood-level community organizing.
- **Policy, Evaluation, and Practice Sustainability:** Conclude with *Social Welfare Policy and Policy Advocacy*, *Evidence-Informed Practice and Evaluation*, and *Reflective Practice, Supervision, and Self-Care*. These subjects anchor direct helping efforts in legislative contexts, equip you to evaluate practice outcomes critically, and provide tools to mitigate secondary traumatic stress and professional burnout.

### Beyond the Core

This curriculum covers the mandatory generalist core that every social worker must understand before specializing. Once you have built this foundation, you can expand your studies across our companion guides:

- Branch into specialized subfields—such as clinical psychopathology, complex trauma therapies, hospital social work, palliative care, and school social work—in [Advanced Topics](advanced_topics.md).
- Translate theory into practical artifacts by building biopsychosocial assessments, family genograms, treatment plans, community asset maps, and grant proposals using [Projects](projects.md).
- Engage with landmark historical texts, critical treatises, and political economy analyses in [Readings](extras/readings.md).
- Deepen your clinical intuition and macro leadership through university video lectures and masterclasses in [Courses](extras/courses.md).

## Curriculum

### Introduction to Social Work and Social Welfare

Introduces the mission, historical origins, core values, and diverse practice settings of the social work profession within the broader social welfare system.

[Introduction to Social Work: A Look Across the Profession (James Langford and Craig Keaton / Mavs Open Press)](https://uta.pressbooks.pub/introtosocialwork/) - Open-access foundational textbook surveying the profession's history, ethical values, and fields of practice across diverse populations.

[Introduction to Social Work: An Advocacy-Based Profession (Lisa E. Cox, Carolyn J. Tice, and Dennis D. Long)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9781071929131) - A comprehensive print textbook alternative to Langford and Keaton, emphasizing generalist advocacy and case-based practice settings.

[Social Work Practice: Advocating Social Justice and Change (Coursera / University of Michigan)](https://www.coursera.org/learn/social-work-practice) - A self-paced video course alternative to the textbooks, ideal for auditory and visual learners seeking an introductory overview of social justice, historical context, and generalist roles.

### Social Work Values and Ethics

Explores ethical theories, professional codes of conduct, ethical decision-making models, and boundary management across generalist practice.

[Social Work Values and Ethics (Frederic G. Reamer)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780231214421) - The canonical text for comprehensive study of ethical theories, decision-making frameworks, risk management, and boundary navigation in clinical and macro settings.

[An Introduction to Social Work Law (OpenLearn / The Open University)](https://www.open.edu/openlearn/society-politics-law/an-introduction-social-work-law/content-section-0) - A free interactive course that complements Reamer by examining practical case scenarios where human rights, statutory duties, and professional ethical mandates intersect.

### Diversity, Social Justice, and Human Rights

Examines power, privilege, systemic oppression, and intersectionality while developing anti-oppressive practice frameworks and cultural humility.

[Diversity, Oppression, and Change: Culturally Grounded Social Work Practice (Flavio Francisco Marsiglia, Stephen S. Kulis, and Stephanie L. Lechuga-Peña)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780190059507) - The primary comprehensive textbook examining intersectionality, historical trauma, and culturally grounded interventions across diverse client communities.

[Exploring Critical Social Work Practice (OpenLearn / The Open University)](https://www.open.edu/openlearn/health-sports-psychology/social-care-social-work/exploring-critical-social-work-practice/content-section-overview) - A free interactive online course covering power dynamics and anti-oppressive practice; use either as an accessible multimedia alternative to the textbook or as a concise practical primer.

[Social Work Practice and Disability Communities: An Intersectional Anti-Oppressive Approach (Elspeth Slayter and Lisa Johnson / Open Textbook Library)](https://open.umn.edu/opentextbooks/textbooks/social-work-practice-and-disability-communities-an-intersectional-anti-oppressive-approach) - An open-access applied text that serves as a specialized case-study companion, demonstrating how intersectional and anti-oppressive frameworks operate in practice with a marginalized population.

### Human Behavior in the Social Environment

Applies ecological systems theory, the person-in-environment construct, and developmental theories to analyze human functioning across the lifespan, families, groups, and communities.

[Dimensions of Human Behavior: The Changing Life Course (Elizabeth D. Hutchison and Leanne Wood Charlesworth)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9781071831595) - The first half of the canonical print sequence, analyzing human biological, psychological, and spiritual development across life stages through a person-in-environment lens.

[Dimensions of Human Behavior: Person and Environment (Elizabeth D. Hutchison and Leanne Wood Charlesworth)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9781071831458) - Companion volume to The Changing Life Course; study alongside or immediately after Volume 1 to examine how families, organizations, communities, and social institutions influence human behavior.

[Human Behavior and the Social Environment I (Susan Tyler / Open Textbook Library)](https://open.umn.edu/opentextbooks/textbooks/human-behavior-and-the-social-environment-i) - A free, open-access textbook alternative to Hutchison's Changing Life Course, covering individual human development across the life stages.

[Human Behavior and the Social Environment II (Whitney Payne / Open Textbook Library)](https://open.umn.edu/opentextbooks/textbooks/human-behavior-and-the-social-environment-ii) - Open-access companion volume to Tyler's text; study alongside or after HBSE I to examine environmental systems, social structures, and community contexts without textbook costs.

### Interpersonal Communication and Interviewing Skills

Develops core interpersonal helping skills, active listening, rapport building, intake assessment, and structured clinical interviewing techniques.

[The Social Work Skills Workbook (Barry R. Cournoyer)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9781305633780) - The primary skills manual, providing structured exercises and drills for interviewing phases, active listening, exploration, and clinical documentation.

[Introducing Social Work: A Starter Kit (OpenLearn / The Open University)](https://www.open.edu/openlearn/health-sports-psychology/social-care-social-work/introducing-social-work-starter-kit/content-section-0) - A free interactive course that serves as a practical introductory primer before working through Cournoyer's exercises, focusing on relational communication, empathy, and conversational barriers.

### Generalist Social Work Practice: Individuals and Families

Teaches the Generalist Intervention Model—engagement, multidimensional assessment, goal setting, evidence-informed intervention, evaluation, and termination—with individuals and family systems.

[Direct Social Work Practice: Theory and Skills (Dean H. Hepworth, Ronald H. Rooney, Glenda Dewberry Rooney, and Kimberly Strom-Gottfried)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780357630594) - Flagship clinical textbook detailing each phase of micro practice, with emphasis on direct client engagement, multidimensional assessment, and family intervention strategies.

[Understanding Generalist Practice (Karen K. Kirst-Ashman and Grafton H. Hull Jr.)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9781305966864) - An alternative core textbook to Hepworth et al., structured more explicitly around the step-by-step Generalist Intervention Model (GIM) from intake through termination.

### Generalist Social Work Practice: Groups

Explores group dynamics, stages of group development, leadership styles, and facilitation methods for treatment, psychoeducational, and task groups.

[An Introduction to Group Work Practice (Ronald W. Toseland and Robert F. Rivas)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780134058962) - The canonical textbook for mezzo practice, providing comprehensive guidance on designing, facilitating, and evaluating treatment, psychoeducational, and task groups.

[Working in Groups and Teams (OpenLearn / The Open University)](https://www.open.edu/openlearn/money-business/leadership-management/working-groups-and-teams/content-section-0) - A free, self-paced interactive course that serves as a conceptual primer on group formation stages, role allocation, and team dynamics before delving into Toseland and Rivas's clinical protocols.

### Generalist Social Work Practice: Organizations and Communities

Covers community needs assessment, asset mapping, organizational change, program planning, and grassroots collective action.

[Generalist Practice with Organizations and Communities (Karen K. Kirst-Ashman and Grafton H. Hull Jr.)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9781305943292) - Comprehensive textbook covering community needs assessment, program development, organizational change, and macro-level leadership.

[Community Organizing for Social Justice (Coursera / University of Michigan)](https://www.coursera.org/learn/community-organizing) - An interactive video-based course alternative or companion, focusing on grassroots coalition building, organizing tactics, and power mapping.

[The Community Tool Box (Center for Community Health and Development / University of Kansas)](https://ctb.ku.edu/en/table-of-contents) - An open-access practical field manual and curriculum that complements the main texts with step-by-step toolkits for real-world community assessments, strategic planning, and advocacy.

### Social Welfare Policy and Policy Advocacy

Examines the historical evolution of social welfare institutions, frameworks for policy analysis, and practical policy-advocacy skills to advance systemic reform.

[The Reluctant Welfare State: Engaging History to Advance Social Work Practice in Contemporary Society (Bruce S. Jansson)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9781337565639) - Foundational historical text tracing the ideological, economic, and political forces that shaped social welfare programs and safety nets in the United States.

[Becoming an Effective Policy Advocate: From Policy Practice to Social Justice (Bruce S. Jansson)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9781305943353) - Builds directly on The Reluctant Welfare State; use sequentially after understanding policy history to acquire applied skills in legislative drafting, lobbying, and policy reform practice.

### Evidence-Informed Practice and Evaluation

Equips practitioners to formulate answerable practice questions, critically appraise empirical interventions, conduct single-case practice evaluations, and measure program outcomes.

[Scientific Inquiry in Social Work (Matthew DeCarlo / Open Textbook Library)](https://open.umn.edu/opentextbooks/textbooks/scientific-inquiry-in-social-work) - A free, peer-reviewed open textbook covering practice-based research, literature appraisal, and single-system evaluation designs specifically for social workers.

[Research Methods for Social Work (Allen Rubin and Earl R. Babbie)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780357764701) - A comprehensive print textbook alternative to DeCarlo, offering deeper methodological coverage across qualitative and quantitative research designs.

[Critical Thinking for Social Work (Keith Brown and Lynne Rutter)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9781844451579) - A complementary practical guide focusing on clinical judgment, cognitive bias, and integrating research evidence into complex case decisions.

### Reflective Practice, Supervision, and Self-Care

Focuses on the professional use of self, critical reflexivity, navigating administrative and clinical supervision, and mitigating secondary traumatic stress and burnout.

[The Reflective Practice Guide: An Interdisciplinary Approach to Critical Reflection (Barbara Bassot)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9781138903104) - The primary text on reflective learning models, offering practical exercises to cultivate critical self-awareness and the intentional use of self in practice encounters.

[Supporting and Developing Resilience in Social Work (OpenLearn / The Open University)](https://www.open.edu/openlearn/health-sports-psychology/supporting-and-developing-resilience-social-work/content-section-0) - A free interactive course that complements Bassot by focusing on emotional resilience, secondary trauma mitigation, and effectively utilizing professional supervision.
